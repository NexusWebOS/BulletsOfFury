Wave Pressure Audit

Every stage's regular waves were played through the real engine on Normal, Hard and Furious by two simulated pilots: a novice (250 ms reactions, notices rounds within 150 px) and a skilled player (85 ms, sees everything). Waves run on their natural schedule with the ambient and adaptive reinforcements on; bosses and minibosses are left out. Every time the pilot would have died is counted.

Corrected edition

The first edition of this page used a filming fixture that, further down its file, switches to a paced inspection mode: one wave at a time, the next held until the last is cleared, reinforcements off. That made stages look many minutes long and mostly empty, made Stage 9 look quiet, and produced a few apparent "reversed" difficulty steps. None of those hold under natural pacing, and they are withdrawn. Easy and Normal also end at Stage 5, so Normal is shown for Stages 1–5 only. Every number below comes from natural-pacing runs.

The game is one-hit-death (shields absorb), so "lives per minute" is counted while the waves are running (up to 10 s after the last one fires). Single runs vary by roughly ±40%; small differences are not findings.

What the numbers say

Lives lost per minute, novice pilot

Below roughly 0.6 per minute a first-time player clears a stage with lives to spare (shaded band). On Normal, Stage 2 stands far above the rest and Stage 5 is the next step up. On Furious most stages sit between 2 and 4.

Pacing

Rounds fired per 10 seconds up to each stage's last wave. Most stages are well paced: 1–28% of seconds are quiet and no gap lasts more than 9 seconds. Stage 6 is the exception (about 44% quiet with a 56–62 s gap on both routes). That gap is not combat at all: it is the Stage 6 team-argument cutscene ("Who stayed behind at base?!", Callisto and, for Cole, the weapon demo), which freezes combat by design. It runs about 58 s, 87 s for Cole. FIRE now finishes and skips its lines (built November 4, below). The carrier assaults themselves pause the stage clock only while a carrier is up, about 10–13 s each in these runs.

Firing patterns and animation ideas

Patterns that read clearly at speed and scale by count and speed rather than unfair aim.

Popcorn jets

Locked three-round burst

Aim locks when the burst starts and does not track: three rounds 0.12 s apart, then a 1.2 s rest. A sidestep beats it. Normal fires two rounds, Furious four.

Ground turrets

Fan with a hole at the player

Five to nine rounds in a fan with the centre lane left empty. Standing still is safe once, then the next fan re-centres. Telegraph: barrel glows for 0.35 s.

Gunships, carriers

Alternating curtains

Left curtain, right curtain, with a guaranteed safe lane that moves each volley. Density scales with difficulty; the lane never closes.

Snipers

Laser-sight shot

A thin line draws from muzzle to pilot for 0.5 s (0.3 s on Furious), then one fast round. Fair at any speed because the warning is exact.

Rammers

Wind-up, then commit

A two-frame flash and a short backward lurch before the dash, with the dash line locked at that moment. Stage 5's rammers are the main Normal killer there.

Heavies

Bullet cancel on kill

When a carrier or heavy dies, its live rounds burst into score sparks. It rewards aggression and prevents posthumous cheap hits.

Stage 2 · Lava

Lobbed magma

Rounds arc upward and land on a marked ring that lingers as a short hot puddle, giving debris arcs a readable landing.

Stage 9 · Water

Sine needles

Stage 9's needles cause every Furious death there. Sending some in a gentle wave (about 0.6 s period) keeps the threat but gives a rhythm to dodge by.

Animation

Fire tells everyone can read

A 0.25 s muzzle charge glow before every aimed burst, a two-frame recoil kick, and turrets that visibly rotate toward the pilot before firing. On Normal, enemies blink once before they shoot.

How this was measured

  • Real game, real update loop and renderer, at 60 Hz: _BUILD_SOURCE/measure_waves_1103.py with wave_telemetry_1103.js, on the wave observer's original setup (wo16Start: native schedule, ambient and adaptive reinforcements on), with the difficulty applied before the stage is built. Bosses and minibosses suppressed.
  • Pilots fly level-3 weapons with auto-fire, invulnerable; every playerHit call 1.2 s apart counts as a life. Hits the assist refuses (firewall or debris grazes) are not counted.
  • "Quiet" seconds have no enemy and no round on screen, measured up to the stage's last wave.
  • Raw data _shots/waves_1103/nat_off/ (assist off) and nat_on/ (assist on); method notes in docs/WAVE_AUDIT_1103.md.
