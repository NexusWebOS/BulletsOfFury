The transferable energy is a huge machine or organism visibly changing the space you can occupy. Its mass, joints, charge, commitment and destruction tell a story. Bullets support that story. BOF can preserve its vertical arena, pilots, weapons and authored boss identities while borrowing this encounter grammar.

## What was actually studied

Visual sampling across the full boss compilation, with closer revisits around the bookmarked cannon and several short encounters. This is not a continuous frame-by-frame viewing or a disassembly of the game. Some attacks are killed before completing a cycle; occasional seeks buffered and those stale frames were excluded. The ledger below records encounter groups and observed systems, not a claim to every possible move in every boss's AI. Exact animation frame counts, collision masks, HP, invulnerability rules, attack randomness and sound design were not established from this review. All numerical lab settings are original BOF proposals.

Systems examined: arrival and scale staging; phase/state transitions; articulated body movement; attack origin and aiming; lanes, fans and radial arrangements; attack commitment and recovery; paired emitters; changing arena geometry; player positioning and posture; maintaining fire while evading; hit feedback; flame/energy ordnance; destruction cascades and fragments; silhouette; palette/value hierarchy; background density; spectacle and difficulty readability.

## Motion and evasion

The runner repeatedly holds a useful position while firing, then makes a deliberate move when a limb, body or shot threatens it. Ducking changes the occupied vertical slice. Jumping changes both height and firing angle. Wall/ceiling grabs let the player work around a large body. Mounted and moving-platform sections constrain the safe area. These are different answers to space pressure, rather than a continuous weave through indiscriminate pellets.

For BOF, translate these answers into lateral baiting, climbing/descending through openings, crossing behind a sweep, and the existing pilot evasions. The active code explicitly contains roll/flip/ram invulnerability, but this lab deliberately tests ordinary movement first. Never make a universal boss pattern depend on a specific pilot ability. Never conclude Contra's exact invulnerability behavior from a no-damage screenshot sequence.

An important distinction: an opening can be spatial (a gate or gap), temporal (cross before release), or an opportunity to disable a source (destroy an arm). Combine two only after each has been taught alone. Avoid covering the sole escape with an unrelated support attack.

## Difficulty and coolness

This player knows the fights and applies strong sustained weapons. Fast kills conceal later moves and make some patterns look easier than a first encounter. The review cannot establish encounter difficulty from survival time alone. What is visible is the premium on positioning, memorized sequences, aiming while moving, and finding an advantageous height.

Proposed BOF progression: first cycle teaches one rule; second repeats it with a different start side; later phase combines two familiar rules. Furious should shorten idle/recovery and slightly accelerate travel while preserving commitment and adequate gaps. Do not introduce hidden tracking after lock. Use the slowest supported pilot and largest intended hitbox to validate actual reachability.

The cool moments have a strong silhouette, excessive mass, a mechanical or biological cause, and a consequence: a neck folds to expose a gun; a body becomes a different machine; an arm stretches across the screen; a colossal hull bursts into connected parts. Add windup, impact and aftermath to a signature move. Increasing bullet count alone will not produce that feeling.

## Art, color and effects

Dark navy/black mechanical mass with narrow silver/purple highlights gives bright weapons a readable surface. Warm orange/red fire and gold-white shot centers contrast with blue/cyan energy. Alien scenes use saturated purple/pink/green body shapes against darker structure. Chunky silhouette edges, repeated joint spheres, opaque fragments and separated highlights communicate scale even at low resolution. Backgrounds range from readable dark factories to much busier circuitry/flesh; BOF should preserve its authored biome and quiet detail beneath important tells.

Do not make color the only ownership signal. In this video large gold-white blobs frequently come from the player; blue-white streams can come from a boss. For BOF, give hostile ordnance a consistent rim/shape, attach a visible muzzle, preserve warning contrast and keep collision footprints subordinate to the readable silhouette.

Effects studied: segmented flame streams and flame tips; bright shot cores with dark/colored rims; star-shaped hit sparks; blue/purple contour and full white hit flashes; muzzle corona; electrical line/jaw accents; movement afterimages; jet exhaust; water/ground contact splash; sequential spherical explosions across a boss; rigid opaque mechanical debris; organic transformation bursts; full-screen palette/flash transitions. Sound/audio was not analyzed. The strongest transferable FX rule is to bind effects to a physical event: charge on the active hardpoint, release at the muzzle, splash at contact, sparks at the struck part, explosions along the failing structure.

BOF already has authored equivalents for many of these. See [INTEGRATION.md](INTEGRATION.md) for reuse and new-generation priorities. The new pressure reel is original art inspired by this vocabulary, not a captured or reconstructed Contra asset.
