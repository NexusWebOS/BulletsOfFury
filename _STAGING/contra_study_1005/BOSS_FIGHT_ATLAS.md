# Contra: Hard Corps — individual fight atlas

Close replay completed October 6, 2026 after Mike requested a much deeper, fight-by-fight review. All 35 numbered encounters have individual observations below; compound chapters and revealed bodies are broken out within their entries. The earlier six lab patterns are broad candidates. The new four-boss choreography kit follows this detailed pass. Every BOF transfer is an original design proposal, not a claim about measured Contra AI.

Method: replay each individual encounter through the actual video, inspect sequential frames during motion, pause between review batches, and replay ambiguous events. Record visible cause → attack → player response → consequence. Separate player weapons, enemy shots, collision-threatening bodies, and cosmetic/death effects. Times are video observations, not game simulation frame timings. The runner can kill a boss before the boss demonstrates its full possible AI repertoire.

## 01 — Maximum Jumbo (00:43–~01:20)

**Review status:** foreground arrival, active exchange and destruction closely replayed at normal speed, roughly 0.4–0.6 s between inspected frames from 01:00 through 01:19. Background demonstration replayed 00:45–01:02, including a close 00:52–00:57 replay.

**Background teaching beat:** robot approaches through the distant skyline, rotates left/right, emits brief cyan eye rays into the city (~00:56), then tall stacks of orange explosions travel across the skyline (~00:56.7–00:57.3). The player stands still. These distant events teach the laser/explosion relationship before it becomes a foreground threat; they are not evidence of the player's weapon damage or a required dodge in that background layer.

**Sequence observed:** distant vertical silhouette rises, launches upward out of view; irregular opaque concrete chunks lift around the player (~01:02); enormous legs descend through the whole frame (~01:03); body sinks below the street; one articulated hand grips the ledge (~01:07), then the circular head and second hand rise; torso straightens (~01:10). This is a two-stage reveal: land below the playable ledge, then climb up to confront the player. It does not merely scale up an idle sprite.

**Attack and response:** the red eye projects a thin cyan segmented line toward the left (~01:12.6). Bright explosions then climb vertically near the left side (~01:13–01:14). The player has moved left, jumps, fires diagonally upward into the eye/head, and returns to the street. The player's selected bright orb weapon produces blue-white and gold-white masses traveling from his raised muzzle into the boss; those large blobs must not be counted as Jumbo bullet volleys. Eye shot and explosion-column ownership is supported by their origin/sequence; the full collision rules and unseen later attacks remain unestablished.

**Feedback / death:** outline/body palette cycles green/red and purple/yellow as the player hits; orange spherical explosions propagate across the torso and sides; body tilts and sinks below the ledge while concrete remains in the air. The defeat retains physical mass and location, rather than instantly removing a health-bar object.

**Why it works:** relative scale is communicated by the city, then by the player's tiny body between huge legs, then by hands bracing on the street edge. The red eye stays identifiable within dark violet/black steel and restrained pink rim highlights. Concrete lift precedes direct contact, giving anticipation a physical cause.

**BOF transfer:** an authored capital-machine arrival with distant demonstration, upward grit/debris, foreground touchdown below the arena, then a climb/reveal pose. A single identifiable eye/cannon attack connects its aim line to a marked secondary ground consequence. Preserve one escape route and stage the two threats in time. A recoil/open-head punish window should be a proposed BOF mechanic, not falsely attributed to this short fight. New FX need here: upward concrete lift, heavy ledge/contact crumble, thin segmented energy line, and linked ground burst. Existing laser/muzzle and explosion art can support the latter two after native review.

## 02 — Blade (~01:40–02:26)

**Review status:** entrance through destruction replayed at normal speed, sequential observations approximately 0.9 s apart from 01:43–02:25. Multiple repetitions inspected, including whole-body and detached-torso configurations.

**Entrance / visual identity:** camera travels up the building to a red-orange, silver-jointed humanoid standing on its crown. It jumps down, physically crushes the parked cyan car, then flips toward the right. Broad shoulder caps, a long asymmetric gun arm, small claw arm, exposed narrow joints and pointed feet make its facing and firing side readable. The camera returns to an arena with two raised side ledges. Dust puffs mark touchdown; color cycling blue/red/gray tracks hits, not a new elemental form.

**Whole-body loop:** land with bent knees, lift/aim the gun horizontally, fire a short colored projectile train; spring up with pointed feet together; travel laterally in an arc or rotating flip; land again on the other side / center. Visible examples: right-side level shot ~01:54, center touchdown ~01:56, left-side level shot ~02:01–02:02. The player lies flat just outside the feet while shooting beneath the gun line, then stands and follows the airborne boss with diagonal fire. Being close is sometimes safer than retreating to the far edge: the gun's elevation passes above the prone player. Landing dust is distinct from fire damage.

**Detached-torso phase:** after a knockdown (~02:05), Blade rises without the gun, spreads its arms horizontally (~02:10), and splits at the waist. Legs remain as a stationary pedestal while the broad upper body flies sideways and reconnects. This repeats from center (~02:11–02:16) and right (~02:20–02:24). A line of explosions breaks the building crown during the first sequence. The runner moves to the left ledge, lies flat there, briefly jumps when the torso crosses low, and returns to floor space between passes. The body itself is the moving threat; no dense radial bullet field is necessary. The last explosions at ~02:25 are the defeat.

**Ownership correction:** essentially all the long orange flame chains in these views issue from the player's selected C flame weapon. They curve as the player's aim changes and persist in previous positions. Do not give Blade a flamethrower merely because the screen is full of flames. Its level gun volley, mobile torso, crushing arrival, dust and structural/death explosions are separate systems.

**Difficulty / coolness:** the test changes from stance-height recognition to landing spacing to tracking a body that can separate. The runner anticipates repeatable paths and continues dealing damage through evasions; that demonstrates an expert solution, not proof the attacks are slow or easy. Reconnection creates an excellent visible end-of-pass beat. Side platforms turn a familiar arena into a different problem without replacing the backdrop.

**BOF transfer:** an original robot with authored leap, compressed landing, horizontal aim, arms-out split, detached-flight and reconnect plates. Stage 1: level lane volley that allows a clearly supported low stance or depth change, plus a separate marked landing footprint. Stage 2: chest module detaches; grounded legs reserve one region while the module traverses a different lane; crossing players use depth changes or jumps appropriate to BOF's controls. Preserve the passage beneath / behind the flight rather than increasing bullet density. Proposed recovery: reconnect pause exposes the waist coupler. These are BOF design choices, not measured Contra timings.

**FX / production needs:** asymmetric muzzle flash and tight shot train; two-foot touchdown dust; crushed prop debris; exposed waist sparks on split; short directional streak for chest flight; reconnect contact flash; a roof-break explosion sequence tied to physical pieces. Prioritize split/reconnect sparks and debris over generating more generic impact rings. Flight paths and collision must share the same transform. The torso can be a separately approved part only if BOF's whole-plate rules for this boss permit it.

## 03 Iron Spider — 02:52–03:35; chase, inverted chassis and articulated reach

**Observed sequence.** The bike starts inside a black tunnel with cream strip lamps. Spider first moves along the ceiling at 02:55, drops behind the rider by 02:58, then repeats low muzzle shots while the rider jumps above the bike (03:00–03:03). The projectile is a small bright tip with a narrow cyan/white streak and star flecks, appreciably lower than the player's body. At 03:04 it rolls upward, crosses above the rider and comes down ahead at 03:06. Gold-spark edged orbs appear above its back at 03:07–03:08 and travel left in successive high arcs; they touch road height and rise again. The source nozzle is obscured by the player's C flame stream, so the emitter socket and exact launch count are unverified. They are present while C remains selected, unlike the player's D weapon; record them as the boss's bouncing-orb phase, not a player weapon trail. At 03:16 the chassis rolls back to the ceiling. Its three-pronged hand turns outward, a chain of round silver/cyan links extends, and the hand swings down to road height left of the rider (~03:19), then across the opposite side, curling back across the front and retracting (~03:21–03:23). At 03:25 it drops and returns behind the rider. At 03:30 the chassis rotates front-on with thin cyan spokes, followed by orange overlapping blasts and retreat off the left edge. Those spokes precede destruction and are not proven to be an ordinary combat attack.

**Player response.** Jump repeatedly over the low shots while aiming the C flame backwards; stay grounded just behind the chassis during the bouncing orbs; move beneath the hanging chassis and aim upward during the claw phase. The close pocket is surprisingly productive. This run does not demonstrate every possible collision rule, and the flames conceal part of the orb emission. It does demonstrate that indiscriminately jumping during every phase would abandon the useful ground pocket.

**Motion and art.** A low beetle/crab body, red eye, perforated silver back plate, three cylindrical legs per visible side, front cannon and independent claw. Cyan metal has broad white highlights, deep navy cavities and thin black separation; orange/purple/red palette flashes accompany sustained hits. Gold contact sparks keep streaming at ceiling and road, tying even its improbable inversion to a physical surface. The body rotation and chain silhouette announce a different attack without requiring a text prompt.

**BOF conversion — proposed, not measured source settings.** An original crawler moves between two elevated arena rails and a ground lane. Ground mode uses low straight volleys; transition locks its destination lane before it crosses; elevated mode unfolds a visible chain and swings its tip through one marked lane while leaving an inner pocket. Orb mode releases spaced bouncing charges that can be crossed between contacts. Make the whole body path and chain tip independently legible. Keep the elevated boss attackable by BOF's normal aiming/melee rules through a lowered core or stagger opportunity; do not import Contra's upward gun requirement unchanged. Provide a distinct post-retraction recovery. Vary which lane is occupied before adding more simultaneous hazards.

**FX work.** Surface scrape sparks (directional, attached to feet), low tracer with compact leading core, bouncing charge with a separate floor-contact flash, hinge/chain release sparks, claw contact dust, and a destruction sequence with thin cyan cracking rays followed by several offset hot explosions. Chain links are geometry/art for the weapon, not an explosion sprite. The original generic pressure ring is not a substitute for these effects.

## 04 — Blimp Core (~03:50–04:23)

**Review status:** road-to-alley transition and all three visible emitter deployments through destruction reviewed at 1x, mostly 0.6–0.9 s between inspected frames. A capture latency leaves an entrance gap around 03:49–03:53; the active deployments are observed.

**Visible sequence:** road ends and camera drops with the rider; the bike unfolds silver legs with red feet and becomes a running mount. A massive olive/brass hull occupies the upper-right, with red structural lines and two curved silver exhaust pipes pointing left. Hot exhaust chains run from those pipes; the running player stays below them. At ~03:56 a red spherical emitter drops on a long telescoping shaft with exposed red/cyan cables. A white/cyan particle spray surrounds it (~03:56.8), its ball darkens, then it sends a short overlapping train of cyan-rimmed white discs horizontally left at road level (~03:57.4–03:58.7). The rider jumps over the low train while shooting, lands, and the shaft retracts (~03:59).

The hull moves above and to the left. The rider runs across to the right and fires backwards into the next deployment (~04:03–04:06). This emitter has a blue rather than red lens during the sampled frames, sprays charge particles, fires the same low pulse toward the right, then retracts. Hull lowers close to the road (~04:09–04:13), rises and shifts back right. The rider returns left. Third emitter drops ~04:15–04:17, charge spray, then a stack of white discs extends vertically below its darkened ball (~04:16.9). Destruction interrupts this deployment: broad orange blasts cover the right hull, propagate left, and accompany a full-frame flash (~04:18–04:23). Do not invent a completed third horizontal volley from the interrupted sequence.

**Ownership:** the large gold-rimmed white orbs grow outward from the player's A muzzle; later small cyan orbs originate from his selected D weapon. Neither is an enemy barrage. The hot left-facing exhaust originates from the hull pipes. The large cyan-rimmed low disc train originates below the hanging emitter while A is selected. White hull silhouettes are damage flashes, not extra laser attacks.

**Player / difficulty:** timing jumps across a low pulse while maintaining fire; use the space under the high exhaust; cross sides during retraction instead of committing to one screen edge. Constant scrolling visually adds pressure, but the key relationship is nozzle height, pulse height and movement between deployments. This no-damage run does not establish whether the lowered hull itself is lethal everywhere.

**BOF transfer:** an original overhead siege platform with a readable hanging emitter. Reserve a depth lane before telescoping down; charge at the actual socket; release a low traveling pulse; retract to create a crossing and punish window. Hull migration changes the attack's direction on the next pass. Let depth movement or an appropriate jump clear the pulse. Separate the high exhaust lane from the low pulse, so dodging one does not automatically collide with the other. Proposed timings and collision are BOF settings, not source measurements.

**FX candidates:** socket-centered charge spray, cable spark, compact cyan leading pulse with segmented afterimage, hot pipe exhaust with bright leading cap and dark red trailing shells, small footfall grit, and hull destruction advancing through authored offset locations. The emitter extension is a mechanical animation; generating a ring cannot replace it.

## 05 — Spike (~04:23–05:03)

**Review status:** perspective-road entrance, close approach, retreating chain punches, second approach, spreading arms and destruction observed at 1x, sequential frames roughly 0.9 s apart. Attack shapes are observed; exact tracking lock and damage boxes are not measured.

**Sequence / motion:** robot approaches from the road's vanishing point as the player runs toward the camera. Silver-blue stacked spherical links form its arms and legs, with red-orange spiked fists, orange chest and small dark helmet. It enlarges with approach (~04:30), raises both fists above its head (~04:31–04:35), lowers them, and retreats (~04:36–04:38). From far back it launches an extending arm straight down the road. Link sizes grow toward the viewer; the lead fist occupies the foreground (~04:39, 04:43, 04:45). Arm retracts after each thrust. Boss shifts with the road's curvature, so the line is not always screen-center. At ~04:51 it leaps upward and approaches again, lands near the left side (~04:52), recedes slightly, spreads both arms, and raises them. At ~04:55–04:57 its arms extend widely into a large open fan while additional spiked balls occupy several heights. Arms return overhead then bend back beside the chest (~04:58–05:00). At ~05:01 the linked limbs physically scatter apart; orange explosions cover the torso and pieces fly independently. This last scatter is destruction, not proof of an additional projectile attack.

**Player response:** run close while firing diagonally into the chest during the first approach; stay to one side during the distant thrust, jump over the enlarged lead fist at ~04:39 and ~04:43, then shift left of the next thrust at ~04:45. Later cross from left toward center/right as the robot lands left. When arms fan outward, the runner stays between the extended chains, then changes sides as free spiked balls descend. Orange vertical stacks appear at the edges during this later phase (~04:57–05:00); do not treat them as the player's C flame, because D is selected and the stacks are away from the muzzle. The precise causal relationship between the free balls and these stacks is not established by the sampled sequence.

**Art / difficulty:** the same repeated link material serves anatomy and weapon reach. Foreshortening makes speed and distance comprehensible without a target marker; raised fists prepare the silhouette before the broad extension. Red fists contrast with cool links; pale joint highlights survive the navy sky and black ruined city. The difficulty changes from one narrow depth thrust to occupying the space between two large moving limbs and separate balls. More bullets alone would miss that escalation.

**BOF conversion:** original telescoping brawler with a distant lane thrust and a close two-arm fan. Thrust: wind back one shoulder, lock the player's depth lane, reveal a thin lane track, then extend the fist; keep adjacent lane space walkable. Fan: visible both-arms-up anticipation, swing the arms outward, leave a central opening initially, then recover instead of instantly attacking that opening. A later version can release a small number of marked dropping charges after the fan, staggered so there is a traversable route. The prototype needs separate body, arm and charge collision transforms and a foreground arrival cue; screen-space scaling alone is not adequate for BOF lane collision.

**FX to generate / reuse:** expanding fist travel streak, small chain-joint spark, footprint arrival dust, dropping-charge flare and tall stacked detonation with an identifiable contact base. Model the extending links in the boss's authored animation/approved parts workflow. Death should scatter authored link fragments with several offset explosions. Keep attack ball art distinct from cosmetic debris.

## 06 — Wrecker: the body becomes the attack

**Close replay:** 05:41–06:24, including assembly, first attack cycle, collapse/rebuild, second cycle and destruction. Normal-speed screenshots approximately one second apart; a few capture delays and the final smaller player view limit exact frame timing. Times below identify visible events, not measured hitbox durations.

**Sequence and movement.** Birds lift from the scrapyard at 05:41–05:43. Loose fists, threaded springs, armor blocks, hinges and spiked weights rise at 05:44–05:47; by 05:48 they have assembled into a tall junk robot with a segmented lower column. Its arms raise and coil above the head. At 05:53.4 one arm unfolds into a horizontal train of separated blocks at standing height toward the right. The player lies underneath it, then jumps as the arm retracts. At 05:55 the entire construct leaps out of the top of the frame and lands on the opposite side; the player runs underneath, then slides left. Another outward arm extension follows at 05:57–05:59.

At 06:05 the body collapses into loose parts. This is **not its death**: fists, springs and blocks rotate and reform a low heap through 06:09, extending toward both sides. The player crouches and fires into it, changes side, then runs away as the heap leaps at 06:10. By 06:11 the tall robot has rebuilt on the left. It raises and coils its arms again; at approximately 06:16 the horizontal block train extends right while the player jumps above it. Orange blasts destroy the remaining column at 06:17–06:18, and the boss is absent afterward.

**Dodge grammar and difficulty.** Raised/coiled arms announce extension; the long horizontal reach asks for a deliberate low posture or jump. The whole-body leap changes which side is threatened and offers a crossing opportunity. The loose-parts phase changes the danger's height and footprint: standing at the old firing position is no longer the same decision. This footage proves those successful responses, not every possible counter or the underlying targeting rule. The large A-weapon orbs and C-weapon flame chains are player fire, not boss volleys. Blue/gold palette changes under fire are damage feedback, not evidence of elemental phases.

**Art and coolness.** The red-orange sky and dark purple scrap silhouette make silver mechanical parts legible. The construct feels made from the actual arena: the same recognizable fists, springs and armor blocks first assemble, then become weapons, then scatter and rebuild. A small red-eyed head and gold vented chest give the chaos a readable identity. The single segmented base and oversized linked arms create a stranger silhouette than an ordinary humanoid robot.

**Usable BOF design — original Scrap Colossus.** Author whole-body poses for assembly, arm coil, extended strike, leap, collapse, heap attack and rebuild. Run `assemble → coil → committed horizontal strike → retract → leap/cross → landing recovery`; after a health threshold, insert `collapse → low heap attack → leap → rebuild`. Give the low heap its own footprint and a visible safe lane. Preserve the player's opportunity to cross during the leap and punish the landing/rebuild. For BOF's lane movement, show the strike's lane before commitment; do not let the extended arm keep tracking. If projectile blocks are separately authored, collide with those visible blocks rather than a rectangle spanning every empty gap. Timings, threshold and punish windows are BOF proposals, not values extracted from Contra.

**Asset/effect work.** Generate original scrap-rise debris, joint join flashes, spring recoil accents, arm-tip contact bursts, heavy landing dust, collapse scatter and a staged destruction blast. The recognizable fists, springs and armor pieces are authored boss/hazard art, not interchangeable particles. Magnetic arcs could help BOF's assembly tell, but are an added design idea rather than an observed effect.
