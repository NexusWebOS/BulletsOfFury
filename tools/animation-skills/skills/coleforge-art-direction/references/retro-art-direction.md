# ColeForge standing retro art direction

Mike explicitly adopted the selected art styles for **all 16-bit and retro games he creates** on November 6, 2026. Apply this as an ongoing visual default to new assets and requested visual work in both retro-styled host games and actual native console/handheld games. Mike does not need to name the reference games again. Current explicit instructions and approved project identities take precedence; an established project's unrelated artwork is not automatically rewritten.

## Shared visual qualities to carry into the work

- **Readable original identity:** strong whole-body silhouettes, clear face/hood/hair/cape/weapon landmarks and meaningful contrast against the actual background. Keep costume, anatomy, weapon dimensions, root and ground contact stable through an asset family.
- **Deliberate pixels and material:** crisp native-scale clusters, consistent outline/shading logic, sculpted material forms and controlled texture density. Use bright cores and decisive light/dark shapes where they clarify an action. No universal palette, outline thickness, sprite dimensions, font or exact RGB signature has been chosen.
- **Expressive complete poses:** distinct preparation, commitment, contact, consequence and recovery when relevant. Large attacks can change the silhouette dramatically through separately authored whole-character frames. Victim recoil, launch, knockdown and settle matter alongside the attacker.
- **Staged environment detail:** give the playable lane room to read; place rich scenery and landmarks deliberately around it. Use coherent environment palettes and clear foreground/middle/background roles. Preserve the camera/perspective selected by the project.
- **Authored flare with meaning:** separate muzzle flash, hostile projectile, player shot, contact, electrical charge, water impact, debris, smoke and aftermath by shape, value, material or motion. Let local damaged variants carry escalation after a short burst. Reserve large screen takeovers for deliberate special events that fit the requested design.
- **Characterful presentation:** let portraits, bitmap typography, illustrated chapters, selection art, result counts and celebrations express the game's theme. Give a major goal more emphasis than routine events while restoring the action's visibility promptly.

These are visual defaults, not a requirement for gore, a specific franchise's characters, the same red sky in every game or a new gameplay feature. Boss AI, combo windows, collision, controls, sound timbre and music style are not automatically established by adopting an art direction.

## Retrieve real images before generation

Use the appropriate installed sheet skill (`coleforge-ninja`, `coleforge-fighter`, `coleforge-run-n-gun`, `coleforge-lightgun`, `coleforge-ninjagolf`) and `coleforge-arcade-studies` for video evidence. Its finder resolves permanent image paths and source timestamps. Inspect relevant originals and sequence boards; supply actual inspected images through the selected generation tool's supported reference mechanism.

Assign roles: **approved project art = identity**, **classic sheet or inspected sequence = pose/pixel treatment/composition**, **specific effect = material and visual rhythm**. A source-game name in text alone is not image conditioning. Keep reference selection manageable and readable; retain the Ninja skill's all-four foundation when that domain applies. Preserve source provenance and current prompt details.

For a concrete art brief record the requested subject/action, source roles, view/facing, native body/cell/pivot, palette/material relationships, complete extents and useful animation phases. Do not invent an exact source animation count or timing from compressed video samples. Review generated or edited art at native scale over the actual game background.

## Preserve authored graphics and fit the actual target

The existing `portable-sprite-games` and `coleforge-portability` graphics rules remain in force. Author complete direction, turn, size/perspective, impact, flash, glow-shaped pixel, fade/smoke and damage variants offline. Do not use runtime limb assembly/stretching, arbitrary sprite scaling/rotation, global opacity animation, CSS effects, additive blending or shader substitutes to mimic the references. Ordinary movement, frame selection, allowed clipping/blitting/flips and camera translation remain permitted.

Retro aesthetics are not a shared hardware specification. For a native target, use that platform's real palette, tile/texture, sprite/scanline, memory, transfer and timing budgets. Re-author detail at the target scale while preserving the silhouette, material intent and pose language. A handheld/8-bit adaptation may need a much smaller palette and simpler clusters; do not force an arcade-sized sprite or a fictitious Genesis/SNES common limit onto it. Preserve original masters and make reproducible target derivatives.

This visual default does not silently switch an established real-time 3D project into sprites or change its documented rendering mode. Use the relevant project pipeline and Mike's current instructions.

## Evidence boundaries

The selected visual studies inspect 2,168 captured samples on 121 contact boards across nine recordings, including 20 denser sequences. They are a whole-timeline visual survey with selected close studies, not continuous playback or exact source-game frame data. Source appearance is observed; hidden behavior rules remain inferred unless separately verified. New ColeForge mechanics and timings must be defined and tested as original choices.

Audio was prepared separately after Mike's follow-up request. Direct audio audition remains unavailable in the creating session. Numerical loudness/peak checks and ready-to-play reference files do not establish heard instruments, SFX, voices, music direction or mix quality. Use the audio catalog and listening deck in `coleforge-arcade-studies` for a later audio-capable pass; do not invent auditory findings.
